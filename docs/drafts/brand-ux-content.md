# Brand, UX, information architecture and content direction

## Document status

This is a strategy and product-content draft for a **proposed organisation** supporting acrobatic rock ’n’ roll and boogie woogie in India. It is not evidence of incorporation, appointment, affiliation, sanction, recognition or authority.

Before anything in this document is published, every factual claim must be checked against a named source and an accountable owner. Legal, safeguarding, privacy and competition material requires review by appropriately qualified people.

## 1. Decisions at a glance

- **Public working descriptor:** **Acrobatic Rock ’n’ Roll + Boogie Woogie in India**
- **Mandatory status line:** **Proposed organisation · In development**
- **Creative platform:** **Measured Momentum**
- **Core idea:** Pair the energy of an eight-count with the visible structure of a governing grid: movement held by precision, trust and counterbalance.
- **Positioning:** A transparent proposal for building participation, knowledge and sound organisational foundations around the two disciplines in India.
- **Primary action:** **Register interest**, not “Join the federation”, “Become a member” or “Apply for selection”.
- **Primary trust signal:** A dated, plain-language status panel that separates what exists, what is being drafted and what is only intended.
- **Visual signature:** Paired motion paths, beat markers and purposeful asymmetry; never flags, national emblems, maps used as decoration or generic “Indian” motifs.
- **Experience standard:** Energetic in composition, calm in interaction, explicit in claims, WCAG 2.2 AA as the minimum accessibility target.
- **Editorial rule:** Specific enough to be useful; restrained enough to remain true.

## 2. Name and status handling

### 2.1 Working descriptor

Use **Acrobatic Rock ’n’ Roll + Boogie Woogie in India** as a descriptive header, not as a legal or registered name.

Pair it with **Proposed organisation · In development** wherever a visitor could otherwise mistake the website for an established body:

- the site-wide status rail;
- the header lock-up;
- the first screen of the home and about pages;
- downloadable documents;
- social profile biographies and share images;
- contact-form confirmations;
- press or partner materials.

In body copy, use **this proposed initiative** after the first full reference. Avoid **the organisation** unless the sentence also makes its proposed status clear. Avoid inventing or popularising initials; an acronym can look like an approved institutional name.

### 2.2 Status statement

Recommended short version:

> This website presents a proposal in development. No legal, governmental, sporting or international recognition is claimed.

Recommended expanded version:

> Acrobatic Rock ’n’ Roll + Boogie Woogie in India is a working descriptor for a proposed organisation. Its name, legal structure, policies, roles and programmes remain in development. This website does not present the proposal as a federation, governing body, recognised national representative or affiliate of an international organisation.

Link both versions to `/about#status`.

### 2.3 Naming migration

If a legal name is later approved:

1. Preserve the working descriptor as a plain-language explainer for at least one release cycle.
2. Publish the legal name, entity type, registration jurisdiction, registration identifier and effective date only after documentary verification.
3. Add an explicit “What changed” note.
4. Update metadata, forms, policies, document footers and structured data together.
5. Do not remove the status rail until the organisation’s operational and recognition claims can be stated precisely.

### 2.4 Terms that require evidence

Do not use these terms unless the exact claim is verified, scoped and dated:

- official;
- federation, association or governing body;
- national body, national team or Team India;
- recognised, affiliated, sanctioned, accredited or authorised;
- championship, national championship or trials;
- selector, national coach, official judge or office bearer;
- member, membership or member club;
- ranking, record, title or champion;
- partner or sponsor;
- international pathway or qualification route.

Safer constructions include **proposed**, **intended**, **being explored**, **draft for review**, **register interest**, **independently organised**, **source-reported** and **no recognition claimed**.

## 3. Brand platform

### 3.1 Creative concept: Measured Momentum

Acrobatic rock ’n’ roll and boogie woogie combine propulsion with control. A credible organisation should do the same: generate momentum while showing how decisions, safety and responsibilities are held in balance.

The identity translates that thought into three devices:

1. **The count:** Eight modular beats create rhythm, sequencing and repeatable spacing.
2. **The counterbalance:** Paired elements lean, answer and support one another without becoming mirrored dancers.
3. **The measure:** A visible grid, labels and source notes make governance part of the visual character rather than an administrative afterthought.

The result should feel athletic and culturally situated in present-day India through real people, places, languages and practical realities—not through borrowed ornament.

### 3.2 Positioning

For people in India who want to understand, practise or help develop acrobatic rock ’n’ roll and boogie woogie, this proposed initiative is a transparent place to learn what is being considered and express interest. Its difference is that excitement and organisational accountability are designed together from the start.

This positioning is an intention for the proposal, not a claim of current delivery.

### 3.3 Brand promise

> Make the next step clear, and make the status of that step honest.

### 3.4 Principles

- **Movement with method:** Energy is choreographed; information is ordered.
- **Precision without coldness:** Technical detail stays readable and human.
- **Open by default:** Publish status, criteria and changes unless privacy or safety requires restraint.
- **Participation before prestige:** Explain access and learning before medals or spectacle.
- **Consent before visibility:** No person, result or affiliation appears without permission and verification.
- **India in substance:** Reflect local access, geography, languages, costs and safeguarding context rather than decorative nationalism.
- **Progress without overclaiming:** A smaller true statement is stronger than a larger ambiguous one.

### 3.5 Personality

The voice is:

- alert, not breathless;
- exact, not bureaucratic;
- warm, not familiar without consent;
- ambitious, not triumphant;
- contemporary, not trend-dependent;
- accountable, not defensive.

Use short active sentences and concrete nouns. Explain specialist terms on first use. Prefer **may**, **aims to** and **is being considered** when an outcome is not settled.

Do not use “we” until an accountable team is publicly identified. Before then, write **this proposal**, **the proposed initiative** or **contributors to the proposal**.

### 3.6 Message hierarchy

Every high-level page should answer, in this order:

1. What is this?
2. What is its current status?
3. Why might it matter?
4. What information is available now?
5. What is not yet decided?
6. What can I safely do next?

## 4. Visual identity direction

### 4.1 Palette

The palette is high-contrast and deliberately avoids a saffron-white-green construction. Colour names describe function and rhythm, not claims of cultural symbolism.

Core colours:

- **Night Ink — `#12161F`:** primary text, dark fields and monochrome mark.
- **Warm Paper — `#FFF8ED`:** main background; less clinical than pure white.
- **Clear White — `#FFFFFF`:** cards, forms and reversed text.
- **Beat Red — `#8E2945`:** primary action and strong editorial emphasis.
- **Balance Teal — `#006A67`:** secondary action, informational emphasis and diagrams.
- **Count Lime — `#D9F257`:** beat markers and selected highlights; always paired with Night Ink.
- **Step Blue — `#243C7A`:** links, focus cues and technical content.
- **Quiet Sand — `#F0E9DE`:** low-emphasis panels.
- **Muted Ink — `#535B66`:** secondary text on light surfaces.
- **Measure Line — `#727983`:** functional boundaries and non-text contrast.

Functional colours:

- **Success — `#17653A`**
- **Warning text — `#704C00`; warning surface — `#FFF3C2`**
- **Error — `#A1252F`**
- **Information surface — `#DCE6FF`**
- **Teal surface — `#E4F5F1`**

Verified WCAG contrast pairs:

- Night Ink on Warm Paper: **17.15:1**
- Warm Paper on Night Ink: **17.15:1**
- Clear White on Beat Red: **8.22:1**
- Beat Red on Warm Paper: **7.79:1**
- Clear White on Balance Teal: **6.45:1**
- Balance Teal on Warm Paper: **6.11:1**
- Night Ink on Count Lime: **14.47:1**
- Clear White on Step Blue: **10.49:1**
- Step Blue on Information Surface: **8.39:1**
- Muted Ink on Warm Paper: **6.51:1**
- Success on Clear White: **7.10:1**
- Warning text on warning surface: **6.92:1**
- Error on Clear White: **7.44:1**

These ratios support normal text at WCAG AA. Re-test real component states, including hover, disabled, gradients, opacity and text over media. Count Lime is not a text colour. Measure Line is suitable for essential non-text boundaries but not body copy.

Recommended distribution:

- 60% Warm Paper and Clear White;
- 25% Night Ink;
- 10% Beat Red or Balance Teal;
- no more than 5% Count Lime;
- Step Blue primarily for links, focus and technical references.

### 4.2 Typography

Use open, practical web fonts:

- **Display and headings:** Archivo Variable, `Archivo, "Arial Narrow", Arial, sans-serif`
- **Body and interface:** Noto Sans Variable, `"Noto Sans", "Segoe UI", Arial, sans-serif`

Why:

- Archivo supplies compact athletic tension without resembling a sports-league stencil.
- Noto Sans remains neutral at small sizes and has script-specific Noto companions.
- Both are open source and can be self-hosted as variable WOFF2 files.

For approved translations, use the corresponding Noto family selected with a fluent language editor—for example, Noto Sans Devanagari. Do not fake script support with transliteration. The font choice does not replace translation, shaping or line-break testing.

Type behaviour:

- Sentence case everywhere except very short status labels.
- Headings use weights 650–750; body uses 400–500.
- Body text starts at 18px on editorial pages and never drops below 16px for meaningful content.
- Small metadata may use 14px with generous line height.
- Limit prose to 65–72 characters per line.
- Use tabular numerals for dates, counts and result data.
- Avoid condensed text below 24px and avoid italic for long passages.
- Never communicate status through weight, case or colour alone.

Suggested scale:

- Display: `clamp(3rem, 7vw, 6.5rem)` / line-height `0.92`
- H1: `clamp(2.5rem, 5vw, 5rem)` / `0.98`
- H2: `clamp(2rem, 3.5vw, 3.75rem)` / `1.04`
- H3: `clamp(1.375rem, 2vw, 2rem)` / `1.15`
- Lead: `clamp(1.125rem, 1.6vw, 1.375rem)` / `1.5`
- Body: `1.125rem` / `1.65`
- UI: `1rem` / `1.4`
- Small: `0.875rem` / `1.5`

### 4.3 Provisional mark

The visual system may use a **provisional Counterbeat mark**, but it must not resemble an official seal.

Construction direction:

- Build on an eight-unit square grid.
- Use two open, offset paths that accelerate toward a shared centre line and then separate.
- Add one fixed beat marker to represent accountability amid motion.
- Keep the paths abstract; they must not read as literal dancers, a national symbol, a letterform acronym or an infinity symbol.
- Use consistent stroke weight and open terminals.

Mandatory lock-up:

1. Counterbeat mark.
2. Working descriptor: **Acrobatic Rock ’n’ Roll + Boogie Woogie in India**.
3. Status: **Proposed organisation · In development**.

Rules:

- Treat the mark as provisional until naming and legal checks are complete.
- Never use it alone on external communications.
- Do not add ® or ™.
- Minimum digital width for the full lock-up: 240px; below that, use the text descriptor and status without the symbol.
- Clear space: at least one beat-marker diameter on every side.
- Prefer one-colour Night Ink, Warm Paper or Clear White versions.
- Do not place it on busy photography.
- Do not rotate, contain in a crest, add stars, laurels, wings, maps, flags or an Ashoka Chakra.
- Accessible name: use the complete working descriptor; mark artwork is decorative when adjacent text already supplies the name.

### 4.4 Graphic language

Use:

- eight-cell rhythm rails;
- paired diagonal crops;
- generous negative space;
- fine measurement lines;
- source/date labels;
- large typographic counts;
- asymmetric grids that resolve into alignment.

Avoid:

- speed streaks;
- flame, lightning or wing clichés;
- trophy walls before verified results exist;
- faux-official seals;
- decorative rangoli, mehndi, mudra or textile patterns detached from their context;
- a map of India as a logo or page filler;
- orange and green paired to signal nationality;
- generic silhouettes of airborne couples.

### 4.5 Photography

The priority is documentary truth.

Direction:

- Commission real sessions, people and spaces only after permission and safeguarding processes exist.
- Show preparation, timing, spotting, footwear, hands, floor markings, recovery and discussion—not only aerial peaks.
- Mix wide environmental frames with precise details.
- Keep skin texture, effort and venue reality intact; avoid hyper-gloss retouching.
- Represent different ages, genders, body types, regions and access needs only through real participation, never casting that implies an existing national programme.
- Caption who or what is shown, where appropriate, and when the image was made.
- Distinguish an illustrative stock image from an activity run by the initiative.

Until original photography is available:

- lead with typography and abstract motion graphics;
- use licensed editorial imagery only when rights and context are clear;
- label it **Reference image—not an activity of the proposed initiative** when confusion is possible;
- do not use generated photoreal athletes or crowds.

For minors:

- obtain documented guardian consent and age-appropriate assent;
- do not publish full date of birth, school, precise routine, live location or direct contact;
- offer non-identifying and revocable image options;
- record expiry and withdrawal handling for consent.

### 4.6 3D direction

3D is an abstract explanatory layer, not fabricated evidence.

- Use matte paired forms, tensioned ribbons and beat blocks to demonstrate balance, trajectory or timing.
- Keep materials tactile and restrained: powder-coated colour, paper, rubber and brushed neutral metal.
- Use orthographic or shallow-perspective cameras so forms feel measured rather than cinematic.
- Let forms pause at key counts to explain a principle.
- Do not render photoreal people, venues, medals, trophies, uniforms or events.
- Provide a static poster frame and a text explanation for every informative sequence.
- Optimise aggressively; 3D must never block the primary content or action.

### 4.7 Iconography

- Use a 24px grid, 1.75px optical stroke and rounded-square terminals.
- Build icons from paired paths plus a stable point.
- Use familiar symbols for navigation and controls; originality belongs in supporting diagrams, not basic usability.
- Always pair unfamiliar discipline or governance icons with text.
- Use filled variants only for selected state, with another state cue.
- Do not use flags to represent language; write language names.

### 4.8 Motion

Motion follows an eight-count system:

- count 1: acknowledge the user action;
- counts 2–3: accelerate;
- count 4: arrive;
- counts 5–8: settle or hand off.

Timing tokens:

- 120ms: immediate control feedback;
- 240ms: small reveal;
- 480ms: panel or route-level transition;
- 960ms maximum: expressive hero sequence.

Rules:

- Animate transforms and opacity, not layout dimensions.
- Entrances travel no more than 24px.
- Keep controls responsive while decoration runs.
- No autoplay audio, scroll hijacking, flashing, constant bouncing or mandatory parallax.
- Never animate a status or legal disclaimer out of view before it can be read.
- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` with instant state changes and static key art.
- Pause looping media and provide visible controls.

## 8. Content architecture and truth controls

### 8.1 Claim states

Every material claim should be assigned one state in the content system:

- **Proposed:** an idea under consideration, not approved.
- **In development:** active work with no adopted outcome.
- **Draft for review:** a visible document that has not been adopted.
- **Verified fact:** checked against a named source; does not mean recognised or endorsed.
- **Adopted:** approved through a published decision process after one exists.
- **Archived:** no longer current, retained for transparency.

Never use **verified** as a visual substitute for legal recognition.

### 8.2 Claim record

For legal identity, recognition, affiliations, people, qualifications, events, results, rankings, supporters and policy status, retain:

- exact public wording;
- source document or URL;
- source owner;
- date checked;
- reviewer;
- permitted usage and expiry;
- consent record where a person is involved;
- correction or withdrawal history.

If any field is missing, either narrow the statement or do not publish it.

### 8.3 Content models

Athlete profile:

- public name;
- preferred name and pronouns, optional;
- discipline and role;
- general location, optional;
- short consented biography;
- verified achievements, each with event, category, date and source;
- image consent and expiry;
- profile owner/reviewer;
- last verified date;
- correction route.

For minors, remove precise location, school, date of birth, routine and direct contact. Publish age category only when necessary and consented.

Competition listing:

- event name exactly as the organiser publishes it;
- organiser;
- relationship to this proposal: none, listed information, collaboration or another verified term;
- recognition/sanction status with source;
- start/end date and local time;
- city/state/country;
- discipline/category;
- eligibility source;
- registration source;
- results source;
- cancellation/update status;
- last checked date.

News article:

- content type: proposal update, explainer, consultation, notice or correction;
- headline and standfirst;
- publication and update dates;
- accountable author or editorial role;
- factual sources;
- status labels;
- related route;
- correction history;
- share image rights and alt text.

Governance item:

- title;
- category;
- state;
- owner role, when appointed;
- version;
- publication, review and effective dates;
- decision reference;
- downloadable accessible format;
- archive link;
- contact or feedback route.

### 8.4 Editorial controls

- Use Indian English consistently.
- Write dates as `25 October 2026`; add `IST` to times where ambiguity matters.

## 12. Mobile navigation and responsive behaviour

### Mobile navigation

- Keep the status rail visible above the header; it may wrap but must not truncate.
- Header contains the text descriptor, a labelled **Menu** button and no more than one direct action.
- The menu opens as a full-height dialog-like panel with a visible close button.
- Use real buttons for expandable groups. Announce `aria-expanded` and connect each control to its panel.
- Top-level routes remain direct links; expanding a group must not be the only way to reach its landing page.
- Place **Register interest** after the route list, not as a sticky obstruction.
- Include Contact, Privacy, Terms and the expanded status statement in the drawer.
- Trap focus only while the menu is open, close with Escape and return focus to the menu button.
- Lock background scroll without moving the page position.
- Show the current page with `aria-current`, text weight and a marker—not colour alone.
- At 320 CSS pixels and 400% zoom, no essential content or controls may require two-dimensional scrolling.

### Responsive content

- Collapse paired layouts in reading order: status/copy before decorative media.
- Convert registers and result tables to labelled cards where columns no longer fit.
- Preserve source, relationship and update labels during collapse.
- Avoid carousels for essential people, documents or events.
- Keep touch targets at least 44×44 CSS pixels where practical, with at least 24×24 as the WCAG 2.2 minimum.
- Do not use sticky elements that cover headings, errors, focused controls or browser zoom content.
- Test long names, translated labels, 200% text resize and device font scaling.

## 13. Accessibility requirements

Target WCAG 2.2 AA from the first component.

### Structure and navigation

- One descriptive H1 per page and a logical heading hierarchy.
- Landmarks for header, navigation, main and footer.
- Skip link as the first focusable control.
- Descriptive link text; avoid repeated “Learn more”.
- Keyboard access and visible focus for every action.
- Focus order follows reading order; route changes move focus to the new page heading or announce the change.
- Breadcrumbs use a labelled navigation landmark.

### Visual

- Minimum 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text and essential non-text boundaries.
- Do not place live text over photography unless a tested opaque surface guarantees contrast.
- Colour never carries discipline, status, error or selection meaning alone.
- Support high contrast/forced colours and browser zoom.
- Avoid justified body text and all-caps paragraphs.

### Forms

- Persistent visible labels; placeholders are examples only.
- Explain required fields in text.
- Associate instructions and errors programmatically.
- Error summary links to each invalid field and focus moves to the summary after failed submit.
- Preserve entered values after validation errors.
- Let users review and correct important submissions.
- Do not use inaccessible CAPTCHA. If abuse controls are added, provide an equivalent route.
- Confirmation appears on screen, is announced and can be retained by the user.

### Media and motion

- Informative images have specific alt text; decorative marks use empty alt text.
- Captions and transcripts for prerecorded media; audio description or equivalent text for essential visual information.
- No content flashes more than three times per second.
- Pause, stop or hide controls for motion lasting over five seconds.
- Reduced-motion mode removes nonessential transforms and autoplay.
- Explain all informative 3D content in HTML.

### Language and cognition

- Set the page language and mark language changes.
- Define discipline and governance terminology.
- Keep instructions close to the relevant control.
- Use predictable navigation and component behaviour.
- Provide a short summary before dense policy text.
- Translation requires fluent human review, not unreviewed machine output.

### Testing

- Keyboard-only and screen-reader checks on every template.
- Test NVDA with current Chrome/Firefox on Windows, VoiceOver with Safari on iOS/macOS, and TalkBack with Chrome on Android where feasible.
- Test at 320px width, 200% text size, 400% browser zoom and forced-colours mode.
- Include people with relevant access needs in usability testing when participation processes exist.
- Automated checks support but do not replace manual testing.

## 15. Editorial launch gates

### Must be true before a public informational launch

- The working descriptor and proposed status are visible site-wide.
- An accountable website operator and contact route are verified.
- Every claim on legal status, people, relationships and activities has a source record.
- About and Governance clearly state non-recognition and document status.
- Empty states replace fabricated scale.
- Image licences, captions, consent and alt text are recorded.
- Accessibility checks cover the global shell and every released template.
- Privacy and terms accurately describe the actual site, hosting and analytics.
- No draft, sample profile, sample event or placeholder article is indexable.

### Must be true before forms launch

- A real data controller is named.
- Purpose, lawful basis, retention, processors, rights and contact are approved.
- Data minimisation and deletion workflows work.
- Separate optional communication consent is recorded.
- Under-18 handling is safe and reviewed.
- Submission monitoring, security and response ownership are in place.

### Must be true before publishing people or results

- Consent and withdrawal routes exist.
- Relationship labels are accurate.
- Result sources and category context are visible.
- Minor-protection rules are implemented.
- Corrections can be made promptly and logged.

### Must be true before publishing an event

- The organiser and source are verified.
- The relationship to the proposal is explicit.
- Recognition/sanction status is explicit.
- Dates, eligibility, registration and cancellation ownership point to the organiser.
- Listing language does not imply endorsement or selection consequence.

## 16. Final creative test

Before approving a screen or sentence, ask:

1. Does it move with energy without becoming noisy?
2. Is the information measured and easy to verify?
3. Is the proposal status impossible to miss?
4. Could a reasonable visitor mistake this for recognition, appointment or endorsement?
5. Is Indian context coming from real participation and practical relevance rather than decoration?
6. Does the next action say exactly what will happen?
7. Would the page remain useful and dignified with no people, events, results or supporters to show?

If the answer to question 4 is yes, narrow the claim. If the answer to question 7 is no, the design is relying on invented scale rather than trust.
